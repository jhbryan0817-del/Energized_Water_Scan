# Current system design

The USV is intended to detect and map electrical hazards remotely in flooded areas. The hull is 320 mm long, with a documented 125.2 mm bottom beam and 170 mm hatch flange. Only the inner E2/E4 probe mechanisms remain. Each uses a dry HS-65HB servo and a nominal timing transmission to a sealed shaft and insulating 166 mm arm. Nominal exposed-head separation at symmetric deployment is 114.6 mm; actual submerged geometry must be measured. Varying orientation is necessary to address the blind direction of a single electrode pair.

The current mechanical authority is live Fusion Rev I; see the [CAD audit](CAD_Audit_2026-10-04.md). The U-shaped carrier replaces the former electronics tray; the ESP32 is socketed on it. The old rear controller shelf and electronics capture bridges are absent. The battery cradle, propulsion mounts/tube, steering saddle, rudder, wet-channel roofs, probe covers and sealing features remain functional. There are 13 documented print bodies in the existing Rev G release. The Rev H electronics iteration did not change geometry. This subsequent Rev I mechanical iteration cleans the exterior hull relief and integrates a Rev H fit representation without exporting 3D files.

The two electrode leads feed protected sensitive and high-voltage paths through separate isolated amplifiers. Rev H uses one ADC per range and wired health/ready signals. Logic and servo supplies remain separate; propulsion current stays in an external fused harness. Refer to the [PCB guide](../pcb/RevH/README.md) for the authoritative electrical circuit, pin map, measurement limits and board interface.

The measurement is differential voltage. A field estimate requires electrode spacing and pose; current density further requires conductivity. DC electrode polarization, fouling, motion, field orientation and probe immersion can invalidate apparent electrical readings. No conductivity, GNSS or heading module is fitted in the current BOM. Expansion interfaces provide room for subsequent integration, with isolation required for any additional wet sensing interface.

Current CAD images are geometric references, not photographs of a built vessel. CAD fit, electrical rule checks and nominal supplier dimensions do not establish waterproofness, electrical safety, reliable actuation or flotation. The [qualification plan](Build_Qualification.md) separates those remaining tasks.
